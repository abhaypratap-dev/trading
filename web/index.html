The rules, exactly
  1. Zone = the first red 5-minute candle of the day (from 09:15): its high and low.
  2. Entry exactly at the zone levels: price rises to the zone high from below → BUY at the zone high; falls to the zone low from above → SELL at the zone low.
  3. Target = entry price + range × multiplier for a BUY (SELL: entry − range × multiplier). Zone: range = the zone candle's high − low (zone 100–110, ×1.5 → BUY at 110 targets 125, SELL at 100 targets 85). IZ: range = the entry candle's high − low, set when that candle closes.
  4. Spike unticked: a wick through the zone is ignored. BUY only when a candle opens above the zone high (at that open) or closes above it (at that close); SELL the same below the zone low. Stops work the same way (the candle must open or close beyond the opposite edge); targets still fill when touched.
  5. Stop = the opposite zone edge. A stopped BUY is at the zone low, which is the SELL entry, so the next entry starts there at once (relay); after a target, the next entry waits for a fresh break of the zone.
  6. Max entries per day: with 2 → enter, exit, enter, exit, then done for the day. Anything still open at the square-off time (15:15 unless you change it) is closed at that candle's open; a time inside a candle (15:28) closes at that candle's close.
  7. Refined data: a symbol's day with 8 or more flat no-trade candles, or any missing candle, is left out of the test.
  8. With 5-minute candles the order of prices inside a candle is unknown, so: in the entry candle only the stop can be hit; a candle reaching both stop and target counts as the stop; if both zone edges are touched before entering, the edge nearer the open goes first.

      How the AI picker works
      
      
        
    1. Backtest everything. Every strategy is run on all stored history for every stock, with costs and slippage.
      2. 
        
    2. Describe each moment. For every signal it records what the market looked like at decision time: time of day, opening gap, trend strength, distance from VWAP, volume, volatility and how the index was moving.
      3. 
        
    3. Learn who wins when. A model learns which strategy tends to win in which situation. Your own losing paper trades are added too and count three times, so it learns from your real mistakes.
      4. 
        
    4. Test on unseen data. The last part of history is held back. The model picks trades there, and its result is compared with simply taking every signal.
      5. 
        
    5. Used only if it is better. It becomes champion (in use) only when it beats taking every signal; otherwise it stays in shadow and just watches. It retrains every night.
